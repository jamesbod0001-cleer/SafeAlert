How the app is used

What is SafeAlert?

SafeAlert NG is a citizen-powered safety network for Nigeria. It lets people report dangers on a community map, hold an SOS button to alert trusted contacts, optionally notify opted-in neighbors, track journeys with their circle, and access curated community resources — without routing alerts through police or government dispatch by default.

Do I need to sign in?

No for browsing the map, reading alerts, and submitting anonymous community reports. SOS works without sign-in via WhatsApp/calls to your locally saved circle.

Sign-in (phone + OTP) is needed to sync your safety circle on our servers, reach opted-in nearby helpers via push, use journey watch, check-in timers, and respond to others' panics.

What happens when I hold PANIC / SOS?
  1. You accept a one-time disclaimer: SafeAlert is not 112, police, or ambulance dispatch.
  2. Your live location is shared while the panic is active (guests: via WhatsApp; signed-in: circle + nearby helpers who opted in).
  3. Nearby users see an approximate area — not your exact home address or phone number.
  4. When you tap I'm Safe, live location sharing stops.

In a life-threatening emergency, call 112 or your local emergency number directly.

What are community reports vs verified zones?

Anyone can report an incident at their GPS location. Reports need independent confirmations from other devices before they become verified danger zones. False reports can be flagged and may affect account standing.

Route scores and insights come from aggregated community data — not official government advisories.

Who receives my alerts?
  • Safety circle — people you choose (SMS/push when signed in; WhatsApp when guest).
  • Help nearby — only users who turned on this opt-in preference.
  • Estate / area watch — neighbors who joined the same estate code (if enabled).

We do not automatically send your data to law enforcement or government agencies.

What we do NOT collect or sell
  • We do not sell phone numbers, location history, or contact lists to advertisers or data brokers.
  • We do not share your live location with strangers' exact address in notifications.
  • We do not require national ID, BVN, or government ID to use the app.
How do I delete my data?

While signed in, call DELETE /v1/user/account (or use in-app account deletion when available). This removes profile, circle, preferences, and panic history tied to your account.

Clearing browser data removes local circle cache and disclaimer flags on that device.

Legal & liability (protecting users and SafeAlert)

Is SafeAlert an emergency service?

No. SafeAlert is not a replacement for 112, police, ambulance, fire service, or any licensed emergency dispatcher. We do not guarantee response time, arrival of help, or accuracy of community reports.

By using the app you agree that you are responsible for your own safety decisions. See Terms of Use.

Are map alerts guaranteed accurate?

No. Alerts are citizen-reported and may be wrong, outdated, or malicious. SafeAlert provides information as submitted by the community without warranty. Do not travel or intervene based solely on the app — verify independently.

Limitation of liability

To the fullest extent permitted by Nigerian law, SafeAlert and its operators are not liable for:

  • Harm arising from false reports, delayed notifications, or network outages
  • Actions taken by other users (helpers, reporters, or bad actors)
  • Decisions you make to travel to a panic location or meet strangers
  • Failure of third-party services (SMS, WhatsApp, GPS, cloud providers)

Your use of the service is at your own risk. Where liability cannot be excluded, it is limited to the amount you paid us in the past 12 months (typically ₦0 for free users).

Can I sue SafeAlert if something goes wrong?

We design for safety and comply with applicable privacy law, but no app can eliminate all risk in a citizen network. Our Terms describe dispute resolution, acceptable use, and moderation. If you believe the service was misused, contact support@safealert.ng with evidence (panic ID, screenshots, timestamps).

Serious crimes should be reported to 112 and relevant authorities — SafeAlert is a community tool, not a law-enforcement channel.

Minimum age & parental consent

Users must be at least 13. Users under 18 should use the app with a parent or guardian. Parents are responsible for minors' circle contacts and SOS use.

Bad actors & helper safety

Can a kidnapper use SOS to lure helpers into a trap?

Yes — this is a real risk in any citizen response system. A criminal could trigger a fake panic to attract well-meaning neighbors. SafeAlert reduces but cannot eliminate this risk.

If you are responding to a stranger's panic:

  • Do not go alone — use the buddy system or inform someone where you are going
  • Prefer meeting in a public, lit area — not an isolated bush road or private compound
  • Share the alert ID and map link with a trusted contact before you leave
  • If something feels wrong, stop, call 112, and use Report abuse
  • Consider calling the victim on WhatsApp/voice first — verify the situation when safe

SafeAlert shows fuzzed coordinates (~hundreds of metres) to non-victims so exact ambush points are harder to weaponise.

Can someone fake danger reports to cause panic?

Possible. Mitigations include: device deduplication on zone votes, rate limits, community false-report flags, reputation scoring, and admin moderation. Verified status requires multiple independent confirmations.

Can someone spam SOS?

Signed-in users face a panic cooldown (typically 15 minutes between activations). Abuse may lead to account suspension. Guest SOS uses local WhatsApp — we cannot cooldown device-local actions, which is why sign-in unlocks broader network alerts with accountability.

Will I see the victim's phone number?

No — strangers do not receive the victim's phone number through the app. Responders see an alert ID, approximate area, and first name or label only. Direct contact happens outside the app if both parties choose to share details.

What should victims do if abuse is suspected?
  • End panic when safe — location sharing stops
  • Report the account or panic via support@safealert.ng
  • Block suspicious helpers — do not meet alone
  • Contact 112 for immediate danger

Medical emergencies & road crashes (Nigeria)

Does SafeAlert dispatch ambulance or FRSC?

No. SafeAlert is a citizen layer — your safety circle, WhatsApp, and opted-in neighbors with first-aid or mechanic skills. You tap to call 112, 122 (FRSC), Red Cross, or a hospital yourself. We never auto-dial government or private ambulance services.

Emergency response in Nigeria is fragmented: numbers vary by state/LGA, lines may be busy, and there is no guaranteed national ambulance app. Plan ahead — save hospital numbers for routes you travel.

Medical SOS vs calling 112
  • Medical SOS — alerts your circle + nearby users with first-aid skills; share ICE (blood group, allergies) via WhatsApp
  • 112 — national emergency routing; availability and response time vary
  • Nearest hospital — NGO-curated list in the app; verify numbers locally before you travel
Road crashes & bad highways
  • Crash SOS — prioritizes first-aid, mechanic, and driver helpers nearby plus your circle
  • FRSC 122 — federal road crashes; may not cover all inner-city roads
  • Witness report — mark a crash on the community map so other travelers can avoid or help
  • Do not move seriously injured people unless there is fire risk — share location and call for help
What is Medical ICE?

ICE (In Case of Emergency) stores blood group, allergies, conditions, and an emergency contact in your profile. It is included when you share WhatsApp SOS — visible only to people you send it to, not broadcast to government.
